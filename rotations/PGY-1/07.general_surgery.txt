GENERAL SURGERY Y1 GUIDE
=========================
Duration: 1 month

Description:
Offers exposure to general surgical procedures, perioperative care, and postoperative management.

Last updated: 11/26/24  
Contributors: Anas Maatough, Natalie Alteri  

----------------------------------------
OBJECTIVES
----------------------------------------
- Assist in surgical procedures.
- Perform preoperative and postoperative evaluations.
- Understand surgical indications and contraindications.
- Manage postoperative care and complications.

----------------------------------------
FIRST DAY LOGISTICS
----------------------------------------
- A chief resident may reach out before the rotation to add you to the WhatsApp chat. Otherwise, contact a surgery intern to coordinate your arrival time.
- Meet the resident team in the **surgery resident room** (across from the OR, past the waiting room elevators and through the double doors on the right).
- The team coordinates everything via WhatsApp and will likely add you over the weekend or on your first day.

----------------------------------------
ROUNDS
----------------------------------------
- **Start Time:** Between 6 AM and 7 AM, depending on the patient list.
- **No prerounding** is required. Rounds are conducted with just the **resident team**.
- **Interns do not present on rounds**. Medical students may present occasionally.
- **Chart Preparation:** Check vital signs, necessary labs (e.g., Ca for endocrine patients), PRN meds, I/Os for patients with drains, foleys, chest tubes, etc.

----------------------------------------
ABOUT THE SERVICE
----------------------------------------
- The **A/Endo surgery service** covers elective general and endocrine surgeries.
- Consults are mainly for **previous or upcoming surgeries** by elective general surgery attendings.
- Endocrine consults are less common but do happen.

**Elective Surgery Discharges:**
- **Same-day discharge:** Inguinal/umbilical hernias, cholecystectomies.
- **Next-day discharge:** Hiatal hernias, endocrine surgeries (parathyroid/thyroid).

PROGRESS NOTES & ORDERS
----------------------------------------
- **Notes follow standard templates** for general and endocrine surgeries.
- Notes are divided among interns, residents, and medical students.
- Hospital orders are typically handled by **surgery residents**, but interns may be asked to place discharge orders.

**Standard Discharges:**
- **Same-day discharge:** Inguinal hernia, cholecystectomy → Ibuprofen, acetaminophen, polyethylene glycol.
- **Hiatal hernia:** Requires **upper GI contrast study on post-op day 1** before diet advancement.
- **Parathyroidectomy:** **Post-op day 2 discharge**. Monitor for **hypocalcemia** (numbness, tingling). Calcium carbonate (Tums) required for **3 weeks**.
- **Thyroidectomy:** **Post-op day 2 discharge**. Monitor for **laryngeal nerve irritation** (hoarseness). **Total thyroidectomy patients need levothyroxine** (90-day supply).

ENDOCRINE SURGERY - IMPORTANT HISTORY CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------------------------
**Thyroid Nodules**
- Referral source (**Endocrinologist vs PCP**).
- Key labs: **TSH, Free T3/T4, Thyroglobulin, TRAb/TBII.**
- Family history (MEN syndromes, thyroid cancer).

**Primary Hyperparathyroidism**
- **Earliest calcium elevation** (often years before diagnosis).
- **DEXA scans** (osteopenia/osteoporosis focus on forearm).
- **Preferred imaging:** **4D CT** (NOT sestamibi scan).

----------------------------------------
GENERAL SURGERY - IMPORTANT CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------------------------
**Hiatal Hernia Evaluation**
- **Symptom differentiation:**
  - **Dysphagia:** Obstruction.
  - **Heartburn:** Common.
  - **Regurgitation:** Advanced disease.
- **Diagnostic Tests:**
  - **Endoscopy** (damage assessment).
  - **Barium swallow** (sensitivity for diagnosing hernias).
  - **Esophageal manometry** (rule out motility disorders).
